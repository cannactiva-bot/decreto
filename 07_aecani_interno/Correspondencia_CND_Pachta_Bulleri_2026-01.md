Dear Mr. Pachta, Giacomo, 
Thank you for the introduction. It's a pleasure to e-meet you.
I am writing on behalf of Cannabis Hub regarding an ongoing legal challenge before the Spanish Supreme Court
(Case 395/2025) against Royal Decree 903/2025 on medicinal cannabis.
We are seeking an expert opinion from a recognized authority on international drug control frameworks, and your
expertise on the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs 1961 and European cannabis regulation would be invaluable to
our case.
Spain has enacted Royal Decree 903/2025 regulating medicinal cannabis. In the accompanying Regulatory Impact
Assessment (MAIN), the Spanish authorities state that cannabis flowers constitute a narcotic substance
regardless of their THC content. This interpretation endangers the free movement of agricultural goods based on
hemp inflorescence intended for medicinal sub-products, or for non-narcotic & non-medicinal applications (cosmetics,
food supplements, herbal smoking products regulated in the EUROPEAN TABACO PRODUCTS DIRECTIVE).
The Decree also creates deliberate regulatory silence regarding products below 0,2%THC threshold, leaving the non-
medicinal CBD sector—which has operated in Spain for over 20 years with approximately 2,000 retail points—in legal
uncertainty.
Questions for Expert Opinion
We would be grateful if you could address the following questions in a formal expert opinion:
1. Agricultural classification of hemp flowers: Under international law, are hemp flowers (Cannabis flos)
from varieties registered in the EU Common Catalogue, cultivated in compliance with CAP Regulations,
properly classified as agricultural products? Does the potential for pharmaceutical use in finished produdcts
alter this classification automatically, or does CN61 only apply when the material is actually destined for
narcotic recreational end products?
2. CN61 exemptions and teleological interpretation: In your expert view, does the Single Convention 1961
exempt cannabis materials that lack sufficient narcotic effect? How should Article 28 exemptions be
interpreted in light of the Convention's purpose—controlling substances with abuse potential—when applied
to CBD-chemotype hemp with negligible psychoactive properties?
3. THC threshold for raw agricultural materials: We understand that determining whether a finished
consumer product constitutes a narcotic substance involves considerations of pharmacokinetics, absolute
dose, and format—matters beyond the scope of CN61 itself. However, for raw agricultural materials (hemp
biomass, flowers, crude resin) to benefit from exemption and move freely within the internal market, a
standardized THC cut-off point is necessary. This threshold is not strictly scientific but serves as
a political threshold for trade harmonization. Considering the European Pharmacopoeia's CBD chemotype
definition (≤1% THC, Ph. Eur. 3028), Czech domestic law, and the approximately 40 jurisdictions now
applying 1% as a limit for agricultural hemp raw materials, what threshold would best serve legal certainty
and free movement of exempt agricultural goods?
Additional Strategic Consideration
Your expert opinion would be central to our legal arguments. Additionally, we are considering whether to seek an
official clarification directly from the current INCB Secretariat regarding the scope of Article 28 exemptions.
Given your unique insight into INCB procedures, we would greatly value your advice on what would be the most
effective way to contact the Secretariat for such a request, whether in your assessment such an inquiry would likely
yield a favorable or useful response for our purposes, and what framing or approach you would recommend to
maximize the chances of a constructive reply.
Procedural Timeline
The case has been admitted by the Supreme Court (Third Chamber, Section 4) with a reporting judge assigned. We
anticipate filing the formal complaint within 20 business days of receiving the administrative file (expected mid-
Februrary 2026). An expert opinion supporting our arguments on international law interpretation would significantly
strengthen our position.
We would of course provide appropriate compensation for your time and expertise. Please let me know if you would
be available to discuss the scope and terms of this engagement.
Thank you for considering this request. I remain at your disposal for any clarification.

I apologize for the confusion. My corrected question would be: Are industrial hemp flowers exempted from
CN61 according to Article 28?
I ask this question because Spain's position is to consider ANY flower of cannabis as a scheduled substance
under CN61, regardless of THC content, and only permitted for medicinal purposes.
However, Spain takes some arbitrary interpretations. Some products are not medicinal and still contain
extracts or raw cannabis flowers. 
The situation in Spain is this:
Cosmetics with CBD extracts do not require a medical license to be imported.
Some extracts below 0.2% THC (such as pharmaceutical Epidiolex) have been
considered outside the scope of CN61.
However, hemp flowers to produce these extracts need to be imported with a Narcotics
License.
And also, hemp flowers registered as "Herbal smoking products" in other Member States
are prohibited in Spain and considered narcotics. In terms of control of this products,
yes there is control which is comprehended within the TPD/EU/40/2014.
All this, considering the new OCM proposal and the fact that hemp flowers are already included in Annex I to
the TFEU and the Common Market Organisation (CMO) Regulation (EU) 1308/2013.
Our main concern is to equalize the situation between raw materials. If an extract below 0.2% is exempted
from CN61, why isn't a flower with the same conditions? This is expressed in Article 3 of the Royal Decree. 
You can also see this contradiction in the MAIN document of the Spanish Royal Decree. During the public
consultation, the European Industrial Hemp Association (EIHA) asked whether all cannabis should be
